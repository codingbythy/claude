# Autumn Rain – Mr. Fox (3-part short)

Vertical 1080x1920 shorts cut from the `glasshouse/` scene on branch `claude/focused-curie-orkd4f` (its loop video, rain ambience and lo-fi music), with narration and centred captions.

`tools/` holds the scripts: `story.py` (script), `tts.py` (narration, Kokoro `af_heart` voice), `build.py` (pixelated HD-2D look, pan, captions, audio mix). They expect that branch checked out at `src/`, the Kokoro model files in `tts/` and Pixelify Sans in `fonts/`.


## Part 1 – Mr. Fox

Someone is already sitting in your armchair. You know it before the lights even come on. You set your umbrella on the counter, and let out the sigh you’ve been holding all day. An autumn leaf still clings to your coat. You brush it away. The lamp flickers on. And there he is. Mr. Fox. Curled up in your spot. As usual. You chuckle, and put the kettle on. Hot tea. A warm armchair. The chapter you left off yesterday. But this house was never meant to be yours. And the fox? He was never invited.


## Part 2 – The Cottage

Everyone laughed when the will was read. Your grandmother had left you her cottage. “That tattered little shack?” your cousins snickered. “Who would ever want to live there?” You said nothing. You just took the key. Because they had never looked past the peeling paint. It was never little. And it was never a shack. Behind the ivy stood a beautiful Victorian cottage, waiting for someone to care. So you did. Room by room, you made it clean, modern, and warm. You work from home now, far from the shoebox apartments you had squeezed into for years. But the best room was the one nobody mentioned. At the very back of the house, behind a door that hadn’t been opened in years.


## Part 3 – The Glasshouse

Behind that door, there was nothing but glass, and rain. An old glasshouse. Cold, leaking, and long forgotten. You sealed every pane. You put in a heater. And slowly, it became the warmest corner of the house. Every evening, you sit here with hot tea and a good book, and the long day melts away. Then, one autumn afternoon, something scratched at the glass. Two amber eyes. An auburn coat, shivering in the wind. You opened the door. He walked straight in. He has had his own little door ever since. And his own armchair. Yours. But tonight, as Mr. Fox sleeps, there is a second scratch at the glass.
